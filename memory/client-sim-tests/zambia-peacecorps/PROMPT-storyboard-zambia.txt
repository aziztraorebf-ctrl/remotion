STORYBOARD REQUEST — 2 concepts on one board.

## THE FILM (real client brief, paid work)

A cinematic map animation about the Peace Corps volunteer presence in Zambia.
We are producing a 5 to 10 second excerpt of it as a sample.

What the excerpt must cover: the state of the volunteer presence in Zambia changes from
its 1995 state to its 2005 state. In 1995 it exists in one province only (Luapula, in the
north), about 40 volunteers. By 2005 it exists in seven provinces, about 220 volunteers.

The client's own words for the style they want: "premium documentary, Netflix / National
Geographic, clean and uncluttered, smooth camera moves, no flashy infographic effects."

## HARD FACTS — not negotiable, not stylistic choices

- Zambia has 10 provinces. The client names seven and explicitly forbids showing any other
  as active: Luapula, North-Western, Eastern, Western, Northern, Southern, Lusaka.
  The other three (Central, Copperbelt, Muchinga) may exist as part of the national outline
  but must never be highlighted, labelled, or filled.
- Real geography only. Real province borders, real positions. No approximation.
- The excerpt covers 1995 -> 2005 only. Not the full timeline.
- Everything is rendered as deterministic code. Nothing is filmed, nothing is AI-generated
  footage. Numbers shown must be the real ones above.

## THE OPEN QUESTION WE WANT YOUR ANSWER ON

How much of the world should the viewer see?

Zambia can be shown alone, filling the frame. It can also be shown as part of the African
continent, with the whole landmass visible and the action happening on it. It can start at
one scale and end at another. Nothing forces the frame to stay where it starts, and nothing
forces it to move either.

Your two concepts must answer this question DIFFERENTLY. One should commit to a scale of
view, the other should commit to a different one. We want to see the trade-off, not a
compromise between the two.

## YOUR TASK

Propose TWO DISTINCT concepts for this excerpt. Beyond the scale question above, they must
also differ in HOW they communicate the change in volunteer presence — not merely in color
or framing of the same idea.

For each concept:
- Draw it as a row of 4 panels, left to right, in time order.
- Name the single gesture of each panel in a few words. If a panel needs three gestures to
  work, it is too expensive — simplify it.
- Put only a timecode on each panel (e.g. "0.0s", "3.5s"). No paragraphs inside the image.
- Draw a colored border around the row of the concept you defend, and no border on the
  other. Do not write out your reasoning — the border is your answer.

Two rows total, one concept per row, 4 panels each.

## WHAT WE CAN ACTUALLY BUILD

This is an inventory of proven capability, so that you do not limit yourself to what you
assume a map animation can do. It is NOT a checklist and NOT a menu to pick from. If the
strongest idea uses none of it, or combines it in a way we have not tried, propose that —
we would rather be surprised than served our own catalogue back.

Two rendering treatments are both fully available, and which one fits this film is for you
to judge:
- a geographically accurate 3D map treatment (terrain, relief, hydrography, camera altitude
  and pitch, continuous camera movement between scales, including from a whole-continent
  view down to a single province) — see reference image A, a real frame from one of our films;
- a precise calculated-geometry treatment (exact projections, globe, data-driven shapes,
  charts, networks) — cleaner and more editorial, less photographic.

On-map and on-screen devices we have already built and shipped: geo-anchored markers that
scale with camera altitude (reference image B shows one of ours in place); annotations
connected to a map point; surface treatments filling a region; province states that change
over time; framing devices that isolate an insert; non-map sequences; animated counters and
editorial text.

Non-map moments are allowed. Changing scale, cutting away from the map and returning to it
are legitimate tools here, not failures of the map.

## MATERIAL LIMITS — what would make a concept unbuildable

- No photorealistic humans, no live-action footage of people, no faces.
- No hand-drawn, watercolor, or painterly rendering.
- Terrain relief is real elevation data. It can be emphasised, but a flat plateau cannot be
  turned into a mountain range — the client would see the lie.
- Every element must sit at a real geographic coordinate, or be a deliberate full-screen
  departure. Nothing floats at an arbitrary screen position over a map.
- Text must be minimal. This is 5 to 10 seconds.
